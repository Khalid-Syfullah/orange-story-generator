## 1. Master art-direction prompt

Apply this prompt to every painting, followed by its scene prompt. Supply approved earlier paintings as visual references whenever supported.

> Create an original, coherent series of Renaissance-inspired allegorical oil paintings for Orange, a modern studio whose story is shared growth. The visual language combines painted human figures, classical architecture, botanical detail, rich cobalt sky, warm ivory stone, restrained vermilion fabric, deep green foliage, and unmistakable ripe oranges. Use nuanced skin, visibly painted brushwork, soft atmospheric depth, and warm directional daylight from upper left. Preserve the same cast, costumes, pigment palette, light direction, tree species, and orange design across the series. Oranges are approximately spherical, with dimpled peel; their interiors have white pith and radial citrus segments. Leaves are glossy, dark green, and botanically plausible. Gold belongs to architectural accents and warm highlights; fruit remains recognizably orange. Compose for an editorial website: expressive art, generous calm space for readable text, and strong subject silhouettes. Generate artwork only, without typography, logos, interface elements, browser chrome, or watermarks. Give every hand, face, branch, scaffold joint, and contact point coherent anatomy and geometry. Keep the imagery painterly across foreground and background rather than mixing photographic hands with painted surroundings.

Suggested shared palette: cobalt `#12558C`, deep blue `#123D70`, orange `#F58A28`, parchment `#E9E1CC`, ivory `#EEE9DA`, vermilion `#A83D30`, leaf green `#32442B`. These are Orange production choices; match the approved images rather than forcing exact color swatches onto every material.

Approve one cast sheet first: a woman in ivory with a vermilion sash, a young artisan in deep green with understated gold trim, and a small distinct group of builders. Use that reference across subsequent images. Never introduce a new face to conceal a failed transition.

## 2. Painting prompts by chapter

Generate each asset separately. Landscape masters should generally use 16:9 at the highest practical resolution, with enough surrounding composition for responsive crops. Make a separate portrait composition where a landscape crop would hide a hand, fruit, or face.

### Chapter 01 — The Model: grow, build, share

**01A — The orange signal**

> A monumental woman in ivory robes with a vermilion sash stands on a weathered Corinthian column in the right third. She raises a single ripe orange overhead with a stable, believable grip. A small group in classical robes gathers along the lower edge, looking toward the fruit. The upper and middle left remain mostly cobalt sky for the main headline. Give the orange warm sunlit peel and a small attached leaf. The woman feels dignified and alive; the column is solid and structurally coherent. Use an elevated, wide, painterly composition. Leave the signal rings to the website effects layer; do not paint moving digital effects or text into this image.

**01B — Grafting the citrus branch**

> A close painted view of the young artisan's hands carefully securing a citrus scion to an established branch with a narrow linen tie. One hand supports the branch while the other guides the tie. The graft junction is botanically understandable, the branch has consistent thickness, and the fingers sit visibly in front of or behind the wood. Ivory cuff and deep green sleeve carry the established costume. Sparse citrus leaves frame the scene, with blue sky behind. Leave a small quiet text area away from the hands. The subject expresses patient craft rather than magical instant growth.

**01C — An orchard that bears fruit**

> A painted citrus canopy with branching structure continuous with the grafting scene. Ripe round oranges hang at plausible stem junctions among glossy dark green leaves. Use varied fruit sizes through perspective, believable gravity, a few small blossoms, and patches of cobalt sky. One foreground branch offers a natural camera occlusion for a scene change. The center-left opening remains readable. Preserve a calm, ordered abundance, with each orange distinctly separated from its neighbor and no floating or fused fruit.

**01D — Sharing the orange**

> The young artisan in deep green stands toward the right against warm parchment light. Two established characters share a cleanly cut orange: one half rests in each person's open palm, with the cut faces angled toward the viewer. The orange halves match in size, peel thickness, pith, and radial segment structure. Their hands and forearms extend naturally from the sleeves. Between the halves is an unobstructed gap, positioned near the horizontal center, suitable for a transition into blue sky. Keep the halves and fingers clear of any future text. The composition communicates shared value through a human gesture. There is no knife in this final pose.

For the brief cutting beat, create a separate approved start image of the intact orange supported on a board, and an end image of the two cut halves. The presentation and handover can carry the story if generation cannot produce a clean, anatomically correct cut.

### Chapter 02 — The Work: craft at a larger scale

**02A — Building the orange monument**

> A monumental spherical orange sculpture rises on a classical stone base, surrounded by timber scaffolding, ropes, and a small group of human builders. Give the sculpture a warm orange surface with delicately carved peel texture, distinct from an edible orange while preserving its round citrus silhouette. It has believable weight and sits on the base. Cobalt sky and modest painted clouds surround the construction. Include a broad clean sky area for editorial copy. Scaffold uprights align, planks rest on supports, ropes connect to real attachment points, and tools remain plausible. One builder checks a stone joint while another consults a parchment drawing. Do not generate checkerboard surfaces, exposed digital meshes, floating ladders, or gears as decoration.

**02B — The craft and the drawing**

> A closer view within the same scaffold, with the established builders and monument materials. One builder holds a broad parchment plan with both hands; another works calmly beside a stone joint. The drawing contains faint architectural linework and a circular plan of the orange monument, without words or numerals. Preserve the scaffold structure, costume details, and upper-left daylight from the wide view. The parchment can later approach the camera as an organic visual bridge. Compose the working hands clearly, without fused fingers, tools passing through bodies, or implausible arm poses.

**02C — Overhead construction tableau**

> A straight-down painted view of the same round orange monument and square stone base inside its timber scaffold. Place the orange center at normalized image coordinates approximately (0.50, 0.50). Builders appear at credible overhead scale around the perimeter. The scaffold forms an intelligible square around the circular subject; long soft shadows agree with upper-left daylight. Warm stone and parchment tones surround the structure. Use a geometrically stable, clean composition suitable for becoming a framed painting in the following chapter. No labels, no text, no abstract machinery. This is the same construction, seen from above, not a new object.

### Chapter 03 — The Terms: shared outcomes, careful selection

**03A — The work becomes a framed painting**

> Use the exact approved 02C overhead painting as the inset artwork. Place it within an elegant dark wood frame with a restrained gold inner edge on a warm parchment wall. Keep the inset painting's geometry, cast, colors, and layout unchanged. Position the frame left of center with generous empty space on the right for an explanation of the partnership terms. Add soft upper-left light, a subtle frame shadow, and tactile paper grain. Produce a quiet editorial composition with no written text.

Prefer compositing the approved painting into a separately generated frame/wall plate. A fresh generation may alter the contents and break the transition.

**03B — Tending the right growth**

> The established woman in ivory and vermilion tends a small orange tree in a classical stone pot on a pedestal, toward the right side of the image. She supports a modest branch with one hand and holds small pruning shears with the other, safely and naturally. The tree has a visible trunk, branching structure, dark citrus leaves, and a few ripe round oranges. One previously cut sprig can rest beside the pot. Cobalt sky returns behind the figure; the left half stays open for text. This is thoughtful cultivation and selection, with coherent tool contact, rather than a shower of fruit or an aggressive cutting gesture.

### Chapter 04 — Questions: clarity and invitation

**04A — The open orange canopy**

> Reuse the established orchard's botanical forms and palette. Dense painted citrus branches frame the left, right, and upper edges, leaving a large calm cobalt opening through the center for questions and answers. Ripe oranges hang naturally along the edges. Avoid branches crossing the central reading column. Create depth with a few nearer leaves and softer distant foliage, while maintaining painted texture everywhere. The image should feel like arriving at a quiet clearing within the same orchard, not a decorative fruit pattern.

**04B — The invitation under the sky**

Keep fingers and wrist anatomically clear. Ivory cloth and a vermilion accent connect this closing figure with the earlier cast. ## 3. Video and animation prompts

Use the approved painting as the starting image. Generate one shot at a time; do not ask the video model to create the entire website or its transitions. Six to eight seconds is a useful initial shot length where supported. Judge the result frame by frame before accepting it.

**Shared natural-motion direction — append to each shot prompt**

> Preserve the painting's composition, brushwork, faces, costumes, materials, and object identities throughout. Movement is quiet and physically motivated. Weight transfers begin at the torso and hips; hands follow the intended gesture; cloth follows the body or breeze with a slight delay and settles under gravity. Leaves bend from their stems, nearby branches respond with smaller delayed movement, and heavier fruit follows with less motion than leaves. Different characters move independently, with small pauses and varied timing. Keep support points grounded and grip points stable. The camera accelerates gently, travels with restraint, and eases to rest. Preserve orange roundness and citrus segment structure. No topology changes, sliding feet, stretched anatomy, texture swimming, robotic repeated gestures, synchronous crowd breathing, or elastic architecture. Keep the final moment calm. This is a silent website visual; audio is unnecessary.

**M01A — Hero life**

> Animate 01A with a nearly locked camera. The woman makes a barely perceptible natural balance adjustment while keeping her raised hand and orange stable. The sash lifts slightly in a mild breeze and settles. The crowd shows subtle, independent attention changes without repetitive head turns. Preserve the column's rigid geometry and the large blue reading area. End near the opening composition, without reversing the footage.

**M01B — One grafting gesture**

> Animate 01B as one slow, deliberate securing gesture: the supporting hand stays planted while the other gently tightens the linen tie, pauses, and releases only its tension. The branch flexes very slightly at the contact point; the graft remains joined. Leaves respond to a faint breeze after the hand movement. Keep fingers and wood from intersecting. Let the shot settle after the completed action; do not repeat it like a machine.

**M01C — Orchard atmosphere**

> Animate 01C with a light breeze traveling through small clusters of leaves. The foreground branch moves slightly more than the distant canopy. Ripe oranges remain attached and sway less than the leaves because of their weight. Preserve the tree's branch structure and fruit count. Use a very modest forward camera drift if needed, with consistent perspective. Show an established orchard; the narrative growth comes from the ordered shots, not from fruit inflating or suddenly appearing.

**M01D — The shared halves**

> Animate 01D with a small, voluntary handover adjustment. The two people comfortably hold their respective orange halves, with stable finger contact and relaxed wrists. Their palms separate only a little, leaving a wider central gap. The forearms and shoulders support the motion together. Preserve both cut faces and every citrus segment. Pause in the separated pose. Keep the camera quiet so the website can control the following transition.

**M02 — Builders at work**

> Animate the approved construction painting with one modest action per visible worker: a builder checks a joint, another raises the parchment drawing slightly, and a third steadies a rope already attached to the scaffold. Motions start and finish at different times. Show believable effort and grip, with restrained cloth movement. Keep the monument, scaffold, stone base, ropes, and drawing structurally stable. Avoid assembling the monument through morphing or moving every worker continuously.

**M03 — Selective tending**

> Animate 03B with the woman gently examining a branch and making one small repositioning gesture. Her supporting hand remains in contact while the shears stay still or make one clearly grounded action at a visible twig. If a clean cut cannot be maintained, retain the examination gesture. Let the nearby leaves settle after contact. The pot and pedestal remain grounded; the tree preserves its shape and fruit count.

**M04 — Canopy and invitation**

> Animate 04A as slow, irregular foliage movement around a stable empty center, protecting the reading area. For 04B, use a separate shot: the artisan makes one restrained pointing adjustment supported by the shoulder and elbow, then holds the gesture comfortably. Clouds drift slowly in one consistent direction. Preserve the steps and sky opening. Add the constellation later in WebGL; do not ask the video to redraw stars or form fields.

Ambient shots can loop after inspecting their seams. One-time gestures should play once and settle. Do not use ping-pong playback to manufacture seamless loops: it reverses gravity and turns gestures into repetitive mechanisms. Prompts alone cannot guarantee a clean loop or stable anatomy.

## 4. WebGL and website implementation prompt

Give this prompt to the implementation agent together with the approved asset manifest and transition specification below.

> Build Orange as one continuous editorial story with four semantic chapters: The Model, The Work, The Terms, Questions. Use React for readable DOM content and navigation, direct WebGL/GLSL for compositing paintings/video textures, and a small Canvas layer only for appropriate fine guides or particles. The art must remain painterly and stable while movement comes from approved video, deliberate camera transforms, and restrained effects. Use the reference recording for composition and narrative cadence, not for copying brand text or faces.
>
> Maintain one persistent rendering canvas and one continuous camera description across chapter boundaries. Prepare the outgoing and incoming scenes before their handoff. Each scene has named anchors, a responsive crop, a stable color grade, and its own small set of motion parameters. Store focal anchors in image coordinates and transform them through the actual crop; never assume a desktop percentage remains correct on mobile. Support separate portrait art when necessary.
>
> Keep five distinct systems coordinated: scene media playback, scroll-driven camera position, transition masks, ambient time-driven motion, and DOM text. Use one normalized chapter progress value and a single modest input smoother. Base damping on elapsed seconds so response is consistent at different frame rates. Start with about 80–140 ms of input response and tune by feel. Avoid stacked smoothing, long trailing inertia, forced scroll jumps, and bounce. Scroll determines the transition geometry; ambient time affects only minor life within a settled scene. When scrolling stops, the camera and transition settle promptly while living foliage or fabric can continue subtly.
>
> Use restrained anchored effects. Signal rings originate at the raised orange and respect the image crop. Give them soft edges, small intensity differences, and irregularly spaced pulses. Keep fruit, eyes, hands, straight architecture, and type free of displacement. Where segmented layers are available, use depth-aware occlusion; otherwise keep effects behind the painting or within clean sky masks. Limit any surface distortion to tiny paint/fiber variation in transition regions. Use one consistent, very subtle grain treatment rather than multiplying moving grain already present in the footage. Keep any chromatic separation confined to a narrow transition edge and near the threshold of notice. Avoid persistent glitching, block mosaics, checkerboard material, arbitrary screen shakes, and digital particles sprayed across faces.
>
> Reuse the same tree, sky hue, monument, and approved overhead painting when bridging chapters. Preload and decode the next scene before exposing it. If it is not ready, hold the last coherent composition rather than showing an empty texture. When scrubbing pre-rendered frame sequences, map scroll to available decoded frames with a deterministic index. Do not repeatedly seek an interframe video on every wheel event; use decoded sequences or a suitable playback approach for sequences requiring precise scrubbing. Ambient video can play normally. Clamp animation deltas after a tab resumes and cap rendering resolution for device performance.
>
> Overlay editorial serif headings with compact sans-serif body text and tiny chapter labels, using properly licensed fonts. Keep all text in accessible DOM elements. Reveal phrases or lines with a small 6–10 px movement and soft opacity over roughly 350–550 ms; do not make every character march in at an identical interval. Time text around the transition so two chapters' copy never competes. Keep menus, buttons, FAQ interaction, and the application form independently usable. Do not render controls into the video or let effects obscure focus states.
>
> A reduced-motion presentation should retain the same content and chapter order with stable paintings, modest fades, and immediate navigation. Normal motion must also support fast scrolling, reverse scrolling, resizing, and chapter-link navigation without flashes, resets, lost focal points, or duplicated texture layers. Return an editable asset manifest, chapter timeline, documented transition anchors, responsive layouts, and a working preview. Separate tunable creative values from rendering logic.

## 5. Fully specified transition: The Model → The Work

**Narrative:** sharing the orange opens a view into the work that creates growth. Adapt the recording's split-fruit opening, replacing its rectangular digital rupture with a curved, painterly opening that grows from the real gap between orange halves.

**Required inputs:** approved 01D separated-halves pose; 02A sky/construction scene; hand and fruit protection masks; exact sky grade; crop-aware anchors for both fruit centers and their midpoint. Keep the two orange halves physically intact.

**Suggested duration:** about 1.6–2.2 seconds at a normal scroll pace, implemented over scroll distance rather than a mandatory timer. For a standalone film, use the same stages across that time range.

| Transition progress | Image and camera | Text and effects |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15% | Hold the sharing pose; begin a small forward camera move toward the gap | Model text clears softly; incoming assets are already decoded |
| 15–40% | Let the cut halves part slightly; reveal a thin curved patch of the incoming blue sky strictly inside the gap | A soft painterly edge follows the gap; hands and fruit remain undistorted |
| 40–75% | The opening expands into the surrounding parchment, following an irregular curved contour; the same blue sky fills more of the view | Carry one light grain treatment through the handoff; no flash or burst of unrelated particles |
| 75–90% | Outgoing hands and fruit leave the viewport through the deliberate camera framing; a cloud and upper edge of the distant orange monument become visible | The Work chapter label begins; the outgoing layer finishes disappearing |
| 90–100% | Arrive at the agreed sky/construction opening composition with zero camera acceleration at the boundary | Incoming copy becomes fully readable; edge effects decay completely |

> Implement the transition as a single coordinated camera-and-mask operation. Use a signed distance field shaped around the real central gap, with a small fixed, low-frequency noise perturbation to make the edge resemble paint fibers. The noise should vary spatially and stay deterministic for a given progress; do not animate the reveal threshold with elapsed time. Apply a smooth progress mapping with zero endpoint velocity. Camera velocity must agree on both sides of the handoff. Use the same crop, projection, and sky grade during overlap. The next shot starts at the exact composition where the transition ends; it must not restart its camera or pop to a different cloud arrangement.
>
> Preserve outgoing pixels outside the reveal mask and use only incoming pixels inside it. Expand the mask monotonically with progress, except when the user deliberately reverses scroll. Protect fingers and fruit geometry until they leave frame naturally. Use a thin soft edge of roughly 0.3–0.8% of viewport width as a starting creative value, tuned for resolution and device size. Do not turn orange segments into particles or create a fruit-shaped tunnel. Force exact endpoints: at progress zero show only the outgoing scene; at progress one show only the incoming scene with no residual rim, distortion, or double exposure.
>
> Derive all mask, crop, camera, and text values from the same progress state. Reverse scrolling retraces the transition geometry smoothly; do not replay a time-based cutting or handover gesture backward. Let the one-time human gesture settle before the scroll-scrubbed bridge begins. Keep an approved settled pose or short ambient hold available while waiting. On a fast chapter jump, use a brief direct handoff to the prepared target composition rather than racing through every intermediate human action.

This transition is successful when the viewer feels they moved through an opening between shared halves into the next world, without noticing a new canvas, a rectangular wipe, a pause for loading, or a reset of perspective.

## 7. Animation correction prompt and acceptance criteria

Use this after reviewing the first implementation or generated shot. Attach the relevant clip and name the specific defect and time range.

> Correct the observed motion defects while preserving the approved artwork, story sequence, composition, and Orange identity. Replace continuous mechanical movement with intentional actions separated by pauses. Fix the cause of each anomaly: unstable contact, incorrect weight, uniform timing, conflicting camera motion, noisy texture flow, or an unprepared transition. Keep architecture rigid, maintain botanical attachment points, and keep human hands and fruit anatomically consistent. Use one supported action per person instead of regenerating every moving element. Reduce the amplitude of existing movement before adding new effects. Remove duplicate displacement and smoothing. Ensure camera speed is continuous at chapter boundaries, text remains steady and legible, and the incoming scene is already ready when revealed. Preserve exact transition endpoints. Return before-and-after clips for the corrected interval and explain which parameters or assets changed. A warped generated hand or changing face requires an asset correction or replacement; shader smoothing is not a repair for anatomical errors.

Review at normal speed and slowly around every handoff:

- **Identity:** same people, clothing, orange anatomy, sky grade, monument, and tree species.
- **Contact and weight:** hands hold their objects; feet and stone stay grounded; oranges remain attached; cloth settles naturally.
- **Timing:** people pause and vary their actions; wind travels through nearby foliage; rigid structures do not breathe.
- **Camera:** no velocity jump, abrupt crop change, unsupported perspective morph, or continuous idle zoom.
- **Transitions:** exact start/end composition, no remaining mixed layer, no texture flash, and clean geometry when reversing or stopping midway.
- **Reading:** no displacement on text, sufficient contrast, calm FAQ center, stable clickable controls, and usable reduced motion.
- **Playback:** inspect real device output at different frame rates, fast scroll, reverse scroll, navigation jumps, and a resize during a transition. Require a coherent hold if the next media asset is late.

Suggested production order: approve palette and cast → generate and review paintings → prepare reusable plates/masks and responsive crops → generate restrained motion shots → build the first complete 01→02 handoff → extend to the other chapters → review continuity and correct anomalies. Keep approved assets and prompts versioned. Do not regenerate a complete chapter to repair one defective hand.
